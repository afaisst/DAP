Documentation

How to use Daily AstroPH

Daily AstroPH shows each day's new arXiv astro-ph submissions, sorted into science topics, and helps you keep track of the papers that matter to you: favorites, your own papers, papers colleagues suggest, a personal "Most Interesting" list that learns from your thumbs-up votes, and an optional daily or weekly email digest. You can also search all of arXiv by author, author position, date, arXiv number and classification.

Browsing daily papers

  • Calendar (left). Click a day to load the astro-ph papers submitted on that day. Use ‹ › to change month, « » to change year, and Prev day, Today, Next day to step through days. Future days are disabled; weekends are greyed out because few papers are submitted then.
  • Heading. The large date shows the selected day, with the number of papers found. The ⟳ button reloads the list.
  • Search box. Filters the loaded papers by title, author or abstract as you type. It also narrows lists in "Your library" and search results.

Topics & classifications

  • Science topics (left). Each paper is placed in one topic (Cosmology, Galaxies, Quiescent, Feedback, Black holes, Stars, …) based on keywords in its title and abstract. The number shows how many papers are in each topic; click a topic to show its papers.
  • Your library (left). Personal lists appear above the topics when they have papers: Most Interesting, Favorites, Suggested Papers and My Papers.
  • Classifications. The colored chips (CO, GA, HE, IM, SR, EP) switch arXiv categories on and off. A crossed-out chip is off. A paper is shown if any of its categories is switched on (cross-listed papers count).

Paper cards

Every paper is shown as a card:

  • Title links to the arXiv abstract page. Below it: submission date, arXiv categories (the main one outlined), and tags such as Suggested by … or Matches: ….
  • Authors: the first three are shown; click +N for the full list. If Hide author names by default is switched on in your profile (under Reading), authors are hidden until you click Reveal authors, so you can judge a paper by its title and abstract first.
  • Abstract: click to expand.
  • Figures: shows the paper's figures in a carousel (only for papers with an arXiv HTML version).
  • Summary: a few key sentences picked automatically from the abstract.
  • Share: copy the link, or share it by email, X, Facebook or LinkedIn.
  • Library: add to Zotero, open for Papers/ReadCube, get the ADS BibTeX, or show a ready-made BibTeX entry to copy.
  • Vote on Benty: opens the paper on Benty Fields to vote for your journal club (see below).
  • Suggest (handshake icon): send the paper to a colleague's Suggested Papers list on Daily AstroPH (see below).
  • Top-right buttons: 👍 thumbs up, 👎 thumbs down, ★ favorite, and on suggested papers a red 🗑 to remove the suggestion.

Account & login

  • Use the Log in button (top right) to sign in or create an account with a username and password.
  • Without an account you can browse, search and keep favorites, but favorites then live only in this browser.
  • When logged in, favorites, likes and suggestions are saved with your account and follow you to any device. Favorites you collected before logging in are merged into your account.
  • The account menu also links to your User profile and lets you Log out.

Favorites

  • Click the ☆ star on any card to save it; it turns into a gold ★. Click again to remove it.
  • All saved papers appear under Favorites in "Your library", whatever day you are looking at.
  • Favorites are independent of everything else: removing a suggestion or giving a thumbs down never removes a favorite.
  • Limit: 200 favorites. When you reach it, a message explains how to make room: save papers you want to keep in your reference manager (Library → Add to Zotero, Open for Papers/ReadCube or Show BibTeX), then click the gold ★ to remove them from your favorites.

My Papers & author names

  • On the profile page, enter your Full name under Author details and press Save profile.
  • Under Author names for My Papers, use + Add name to add the other ways your name appears on papers (for example "V. C. Rubin" or a maiden name).
  • Papers of the selected day with an author matching one of these names (same surname and first initial) are collected in My Papers and highlighted with a gold border.
  • Matching uses names only. The ORCID field is stored on your profile but not used for matching, because arXiv's daily listing does not include ORCIDs.

Hiding author names

  • To judge papers by their title and abstract without being influenced by who wrote them, switch on Hide author names by default on the profile page (under Reading) and press Save profile.
  • Paper cards then show Authors hidden (N) with a Reveal authors button. Revealing shows that one paper's authors; the others stay hidden.
  • This applies everywhere papers are listed: daily topics, Your library and arXiv search results. Revealed papers stay revealed until you reload the page.
  • The setting is saved with your account, so it works on every device you log in on. My Papers and the search box still find papers by author.

Suggesting papers

  • Click Suggest (handshake icon) on a paper card, type part of a colleague's name or username, pick them and press Suggest (you need to be logged in).
  • The paper then appears in their Suggested Papers list, tagged with who suggested it.
  • To remove a suggestion from your list, click the red trash bin on its card and confirm. This cannot be undone, but if you also starred the paper it stays in your Favorites.
  • Limit: 200 suggested papers. When your list is full, colleagues can't suggest new papers to you (they see a message), and you get a notice. Make room by moving papers you want to keep to your Favorites (click ☆) and removing suggestions with the trash bin.

Thumbs up / down & Most Interesting

  • 👍 Thumbs up (blue): tells Daily AstroPH you like this kind of paper. It extracts about a dozen distinctive keywords and phrases from the title and abstract (e.g. "rapid quenching", "jwst") and remembers them. A short message at the bottom shows what was learned.
  • 👎 Thumbs down (grey): "not interesting". The paper is hidden from Most Interesting, and its keywords count against similar papers.
  • Clicking an active thumb again removes the vote; up and down switch between each other. You need to be logged in.
  • Most Interesting (Your library) lists the selected day's papers that best match your liked keywords, best first. Title matches count double. The blue Matches: … tag on each card shows why it was picked. The first 12 are shown; Show more reveals the rest. It respects the Classifications chips and the search box.
  • Your learned keywords are listed on the profile page under What you like (and Less interested in), together with all rated papers. Forget everything deletes them and starts over.

Searching all of arXiv

Click the magnifier (top right) to search the whole arXiv, not only today's papers. Fill in any combination of fields; all filled fields must match:

  • First author: e.g. "Rubin, V", "V. Rubin" or just "Rubin".
  • Author and at author position between: finds papers where that person is at the given author positions. "4 – 6" means 4th, 5th or 6th author; leave a box empty for no limit on that side (e.g. "4 – " for 4th or later), or both empty for any position.
  • Submitted from / until: limit to a date range (either end can be left empty).
  • arXiv number(s): one or more IDs separated by commas, e.g. "2610.20722, 2501.01234". Versions (v2) are ignored.
  • Only astro-ph papers (on by default) restricts the search to astro-ph, and the Classifications chips below it (same colors as on the main page) narrow it to e.g. only GA and CO. Uncheck it to search all of arXiv.

Results appear under arXiv search at the top of the left column, with Edit search and Clear search buttons. Each search checks up to the 300 most recent arXiv matches; for common surnames, add a date range to reach older papers.

Voting on Benty Fields

  • Vote on Benty opens the paper's page on Benty Fields, where you can vote for it in your journal club.
  • You need to be logged in to Benty Fields in the same browser. The vote itself happens on Benty Fields; Daily AstroPH cannot vote for you.

Email digest

  • On the profile page, enter your email address under Email digest, choose Daily or Weekly and press Save profile.
  • Daily: Monday–Friday at 7 am US Pacific, the 12 most interesting papers from the latest arXiv batch (announced the evening before).
  • Weekly: Mondays at 7 am US Pacific, the 30 most interesting papers from the previous week.
  • Papers are ranked by your thumbs-up and thumbs-down votes, like the Most Interesting list, across all astro-ph classifications. If you haven't liked any paper yet, no digest is sent.
  • Each paper in the email shows its title, first authors, arXiv link and the keywords it matched.
  • To stop the emails, set the digest to Off, or use the Unsubscribe link at the bottom of any digest (it opens a page where you confirm with one click) or your mail app's own Unsubscribe button.
  • Not receiving it? Some mail systems, especially university and Outlook/Microsoft 365 accounts, put the digest in the junk folder or hold it back. Add dailyastrojournal@gmail.com to your safe senders or contacts and mark the email as "Not junk" if it lands there. A Gmail address usually works best for receiving the digest.

Profile page

Open it from the account menu (top right → User profile). It has three boxes:

  • Profile settings: author details, author names for My Papers, reading preferences (hide author names) and the email digest. Save profile becomes active as soon as you change something and shows a green "✓ Profile saved" when done. Delete account is at the bottom of this box.
  • Password: Change password becomes active once all three fields are filled and the new passwords match.
  • What you like: what the app learned from your thumbs up/down, with Forget everything.

In short, you can:

  • set your full name and ORCID, and the name variants used for My Papers;
  • hide author names by default;
  • set your email address and daily/weekly digest;
  • see and reset what the app learned from your thumbs up/down (What you like);
  • change your password;
  • delete your account and all its saved data.

Header buttons, dark mode & shortcuts

  • The buttons at the top right are, from left to right: ? this help page, the magnifier for searching all of arXiv, the moon / sun for dark mode, and your account menu (log in, profile, admin, log out).
  • The moon / sun button switches between light and dark mode. By default the page follows your computer's setting; your choice is remembered in this browser.
  • Keyboard: ← / → previous / next day, / jump to the search box.

For admins

Admin accounts see an Admin link in the account menu. The Admin Tools page shows:

  • Email Digest: whether sending is set up and scheduled, the number of daily and weekly subscribers, Preview buttons that show your own daily or weekly digest without sending it, and Send test buttons that email it to the address on your profile.
  • Member List: a table of all accounts with username, full name, ORCID, email address, digest setting (daily / weekly / off), number of favorites and creation date. Search it by username, name, email or ORCID, and click a column header to sort (click again to reverse). The last two columns hold the Co-admin checkbox and the Delete button.
  • ArXiv Requests: how often the server asked arXiv for data, per hour, for the last 2, 7 or 14 days (hover over a bar for details). It also shows how many requests were answered from the server's memory, how often arXiv answered "too many requests" (red dots), and whether the server is currently pausing requests. A daily table lists the same numbers per purpose (daily lists, search, figures, email digest). Statistics are kept for 14 days.
  • Co-admins: the main admin can tick Co-admin on any member to give them admin rights. Co-admins can use everything on the admin page, but cannot add or remove co-admins, and cannot delete the main admin or other co-admins.

Good to know

  • Dates are submission dates. arXiv announces papers the next weekday evening, so a paper submitted on Monday appears in arXiv's Tuesday listing.
  • arXiv limits requests. If arXiv is busy you may see "arXiv is limiting requests right now"; wait a few minutes and press ⟳. Days that were loaded recently are kept by the server and show up instantly.
  • Limits. Each account can keep up to 200 favorites and 200 suggested papers, to keep the app's database small. See Favorites and Suggesting papers.
  • Topics are automatic. Topic assignment and summaries are keyword-based guesses, not editorial choices.
